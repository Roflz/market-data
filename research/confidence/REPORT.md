# Confidence scores for pattern entries and exits: walk-forward results

15-minute bars; ES, NQ and YM traded as MES, MNQ and MYM. All money is **net $ per 1 micro contract after costs**: $1.00 commission, 1 tick of slippage on entry and on exit, and 1 more tick when a stop fills. Results are **out of sample**: every number for a year comes from a model trained only on earlier years. The test years are 2013 to 2024 and the first half of 2025. The locked holdout (2025-07-01 onward) was never labelled, fitted or looked at. Generated by `run_pipeline.py` on 2026-11-08.

## The short version

* **Entries.** The chosen confidence model (pooled logistic regression) kept 7.1% of the 703,597 test events. Its trades averaged **-$2.25 per trade** (-$9,027 a year at 1 micro each). Taking every event averaged -$3.23, and random skipping to the same count averaged -$3.16 (5-95%: -$3.44 to -$2.89). The score ranks trades well (AUC 0.614), but the selected trades still loses money after costs.
* The best row of all 5 entry runs is pooled gbm at -$0.65 per trade. The count of runs matters, see section 7. No entry configuration made money out of sample.
* **Data-snooping count:** 9 out-of-sample configurations are logged in `out/trials.csv`.

## What was tested

* **Entry events.** An event is any 15-minute bar where at least one of the 24 textbook directional candlestick patterns fires. The trade goes in the pattern's textbook direction. The patterns fire often, so there are about 80 events per market per day.
* **The trade.** Enter at the open of the next bar. The stop goes 1 tick beyond the pattern's extreme. The target is 1.5 times the risk (1.5R). The time limit is 16 bars (4 hours) or 16:45 ET, whichever comes first. The path is checked minute by minute; if one minute touches both the stop and the target, the stop counts. A trade is a **win** (y = 1) when it makes money after costs.
* **The confidence model.** For every event the model estimates the chance the trade is a win, using everything known at the close of the signal bar: all patterns, pattern families, swing structure, EMAs, momentum, VWAP, the bar's shape and volume, the day so far, time of day, the 60-minute trend and the other two markets. Every input is seen from the trade's side, so + means "in the trade's direction".
  * `logit` is the main model: a regularised logistic regression whose weights become the **scorecard**.
  * `gbm` is shallow boosted trees with isotonic calibration.
  * `vote` is an equal-weight family vote. It is the simple baseline the learned models must beat.
* **Decision.** Take an event when its score is at or above a threshold. Each fold picks its threshold on its own training years (out-of-sample predictions there) to maximise $ per trade, keeping at least 5% of events. The test year never sets its own threshold.
* **Walk-forward.** Expanding window by calendar year. Labels that end within 1 day of the test start are purged. YM data starts in 2018, so YM's own folds start in 2021. Events are weighted by how much their trade windows overlap and by how many markets fire on the same bar, since ES/NQ/YM events on the same bar are close to one trade.
* **Exit model.** Trades from 25% of the entry events plus random-time entries are held with the stop only (no target), up to 32 bars or 16:45. At the close of every bar the trade is open, the model scores "exit at the next open beats holding to the end". The learned exit closes the trade at the first bar where that score clears the fold's threshold.
* **Units.** Distances are in **average bars**: 1 average bar is the mean high-low range of the 20 bars before the signal. R is the trade's initial risk (entry to stop).
* **Two inputs added beyond SPEC.md** (both known at the signal close): `stop_R20` is the stop distance in average bars, and `cost_R` is the round-trip cost as a share of the planned risk. The pooled models also get a market flag (`mkt_ES/NQ/YM`).

## 3. Could it be luck? Permutation test

The outcomes (win/loss and $) were shuffled at random within each year, which destroys any real link between the inputs and the outcome. The whole procedure (model, C, threshold) was then refitted, and the shuffled model was scored on the **true** test outcomes. A real edge should beat almost every shuffle. With 20 shuffles the smallest possible p-value is 1/21 = 0.048.

(not run)

## 4. Are the weights stable from year to year?

Each fold refits the logistic regression. *Sign stability* is the share of folds in which a weight has the same sign as its median. 1.0 means it always points the same way; 0.5 means it flips half the time.

* **entry model (pooled)**: 87 inputs, 13 folds. 35 inputs keep their sign in every fold, and 53 do in at least 80% of folds. Of the 3 inputs with a sizeable weight (|weight per 1 SD| >= 0.05), 3 are stable in at least 80% of folds.

## 5. The scorecards

**How to read a scorecard.** Every input has a number of points per unit, taken from the median logistic weight across the folds.

* A trade's score is the sum of points times the input's value.
* **+20 points doubles the odds** (odds = chance / (1 - chance)), and -20 halves them. For example, odds of 1:2 (a 33% chance) become 2:2 (50%) at +20 points.
* Signed inputs are seen from the trade, so "+1" means "in the trade's direction": a bullish pattern on a long, a bearish one on a short.
* *Points per 1 SD* gives the points for a typical-size change in the input, which lets you compare inputs measured in different units.
* *Stable* is the sign stability defined in section 4.

## 6. Prop-firm odds: LucidFlex 25K

The chosen entry rule's out-of-sample trades are summed per trading day at **1 micro per trade**. That daily $ series is run through `prop_sim.simulate` with the LucidFlex 25K defaults:

* $1,250 profit target.
* $1,000 maximum loss, trailing the highest end-of-day balance and locking at $25,100.
* 40% consistency rule.

An account is started on every trading day of the test period. **Pass odds** = passes / (passes + fails); accounts still open when the data ends are left out.

**Day-block views.** These rebuild 200 alternative histories from runs of 5 or 20 *consecutive* trading days of the real series, then start an account on every day of each history. Keeping days in blocks keeps losing streaks together; shuffling single trades would break them up and make passing look easier. The table shows the median pass rate with its 5-95% range.

## 7. Caveats and the data-snooping count

* **Trials.** `out/trials.csv` logs 9 model configurations evaluated out of sample. These are 7 entry and 2 exit walk-forwards: 3 models x 4 scopes, plus 3 two-fold timing tests run before the full run. Permutation and prop runs are logged too, but they are tests of those configurations, not new ones. The earlier single-pattern, filter and timeframe studies in `research/signals` also count against this data, and they touched 2013-2025. Treat a borderline result as not proven.
* **The chosen model was fixed in advance.** It is the pooled logit, because it produces the scorecard. The other models and scopes are shown for comparison, not picked by their results.
* **Prices of the day.** $ are at the prices of the time, with fixed $ costs. The same move is worth fewer $ in 2013 than in 2025, so costs weigh more in early years. The models see costs through `cost_R`.
* **No position limit in the labels.** Every event is its own trade, so overlapping trades in the same or another market all count. Section 6 shows how many are open at once.
* **Exits are judged on the baseline trade** (stop only, no target). A learned exit that beats holding is not automatically better than the 1.5R target used for entries.
* **Module fix made during this run.** scikit-learn 1.9's HistGradientBoosting crashes on a feature column with no finite value in the training rows. Pooled folds trained before 2018 hit this, because YM's cross-market columns are empty then. `walkforward.GBMModel` now fits only on columns that vary in the training rows (`informative_columns`); regression test `test_gbm_constant_and_empty_columns` in `test_walkforward.py`.
* The locked holdout (Globex sessions from 2025-07-01) was cut from the bars before any labelling.
