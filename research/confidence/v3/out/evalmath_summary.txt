1. Calibration: zero-edge Gaussian days at daily sd $350-500 pass within 10 days 10-11% (40% rule) = the v2 luck bar 8-11%.
2. STRUCTURE BEATS THE OLD LUCK BAR WITHOUT ANY EDGE. Real pre-holdout random trades (luck pools, real costs) with
   "fixed $ risk $250-400/trade, up to 2-3 trades/day, only take a trade whose stop fits in the $600 daily room,
   stop for the day once green" pass within 10 days 18-22% (MES 18, MNQ 22, MYM 21; 2018+ starts, 40% rule),
   vs 5-7% (random side) / 11-14% (always long) without the stop-once-green rule. Idealized two-point trades: 32%.
   => the honest luck control for any strategy is its zero-edge twin with the SAME day management (~20% pass10,
   ~22-28% eventual pass rate), not the v2 bar.
3. Speed is cheap, pass PROBABILITY needs edge. With zero edge the eventual pass rate is capped near 33-36% by the
   geometry ($1,000 drawdown vs $1,250 target); edge raises it: net expectancy per trade +0.05R -> 44-53%,
   +0.10R -> 54-74%, +0.20R -> 72-95% (fast vs slow sizing). Pass within 10 days: 32% (0R) -> 37% (0.05R)
   -> 45% (0.10R) -> 63% (0.20R).
4. Gaussian view: daily Sharpe (mean/sd of daily $) 0.10 -> 1.55x luck within 10 days, 0.15 -> 1.9x, 0.20 -> 2.3x,
   0.30 -> 3.1x; best daily sd $400 (40% rule) / $500 (50% rule). Annualized: 0.15 daily = 2.4.
5. Skew: under the 40% consistency rule + trailing drawdown + $600 daily limit, high-win-rate / small-target trades
   beat lottery trades at the same mean & sd (pass10 22.6% vs 11.5% at win rate 85% vs 15%), and b=1R targets beat
   3R targets at the same R-expectancy. Best DAY shape: upside capped near $500 (= 40% of target), downside limited
   by the daily room; i.e. stop once green / at +$500. Stopping after one red trade HURTS (keep the room).
6. Combining weak edges: combined daily Sharpe = s*sqrt(K)/sqrt(1+(K-1)rho). 16 uncorrelated s=0.05 streams -> 0.20
   (pass20 26% -> 48%); with rho=0.3 it saturates near 0.09 (useless). Each stream must be scaled DOWN so total daily
   sd stays $300-450; stacking at full size pushes sd past the optimum and lowers pass odds.
7. Sizing: fixed $ risk per trade (micros = $B / stop $ per micro) > fixed micros (11.7 vs 18.1% pass10 at +0.1R) >
   trailing-vol targeting (lags). Cushion-proportional sizing never busts but stalls (zombie accounts). Scale-up after
   gains: worse; scale-down after +$500: higher pass rate, slower. Daily profit stop is the biggest lever (+20 pts).
   $250-300 risk/trade for speed (median ~6 days); $150 for pass probability (69% at +0.1R, median 13 days).
8. Event-only edges (<= 1 day/month) cannot drive a fast pass (0-5% within 10 days even at per-day Sharpe 0.8).
   Positive autocorrelation of daily P&L helps (phi 0.4: pass10 13 -> 24% at S 0.1); negative hurts.
9. Modeling caveats: flooring the day's END P&L at -$600 (prop_sim / eval_sim style) adds a phantom edge for wide
   days (+$3/day at sd 300, +$12 at 400, +$50 at 600 for a zero-edge stream) - A/C/D here use an exact Brownian
   intraday stop instead. Enforcing the trailing drawdown intraday (real-time) costs <= 1 pt of pass10 but +8-10 pts
   of bust-within-20 at sd $250-500.
TARGET SPEC: net >= +0.10R per trade after costs, beyond the plain-drift control, in both halves and 2022+ (MES 60-min
stops ~$59/micro -> >= $6/micro/trade; MES 15-min >= $3; MNQ 60-min >= $10, 15-min >= $4.5; MYM 60-min >= $4.5);
>= 1 trade on >= 80% of days (2-3 chances/day ideal); win rate >= 45% with 1-1.5R targets; daily Sharpe of the managed
stream >= 0.15; top-10-days removal keeps most of the profit; proof needs t >= 3 on ~1,400-2,300 trades at 0.10R
