# Section 12: NQ vs ES relative value (rules S1, S2, S3)

Pre-registered before any P&L was seen. Implement EXACTLY as written; any ambiguity: choose the most conservative reading, write it down under 'deviations', and do not tune anything.


## Summary from the research stage
After review, no rule in this family has better than weak evidence. No primary source tests an NQ-vs-ES spread trade net of costs, and every cited sample ends in 2020 or earlier except a practitioner paper that does not test NQ/ES. Three specs can still be tested cheaply on our data. Ranked best first: (1) a calm-day intraday fade of the beta-hedged NQ-ES residual from 14:30 to 16:00, the only one that targets chop days; (2) monthly NQ-ES spread momentum, which is market-neutral, has negligible costs and does not care about intraday trend or chop; (3) a big-day continuation trade on the 15:30-16:00 leveraged-ETF close flow, kept because the reviewer advised testing it, but expected to fail net of costs. The original calm-day rule fired only about 4 times a year in our data, so its threshold was loosened to |z| >= 1 (about 24 a year). Thresholds were set from signal counts only, with no P&L viewed. The reviewer's overlap objection is resolved: spec 3 gives way to spec 1. A two-leg micro round trip costs about 11 bps at 2010 index levels and under 2 bps now, so both intraday specs face a much higher cost bar early in the sample.

## S1 Calm-day NQ/ES intraday residual fade, 14:30 to 16:00 (MNQ vs MES)

### mechanism
On calm, low-VIX days with no scheduled macro release and no overnight NQ-specific news, a large beta-hedged NQ-vs-ES gap built between 9:30 and 14:30 is more likely flow or inventory than information. If options dealers are net long gamma, their hedging leans against the move and part of the gap closes by the 16:00 cash close. The support is indirect only. (a) Adams et al., SSRN 5641974, read via a Quantpedia summary: higher market-maker net gamma 'predicts a negative momentum in high-frequency returns'; the related E-mini order-flow reversal and volatility effects dissipate after about one hour. This evidence covers S&P/E-mini only, with no NDX, and the sample end is not stated. (b) Our own replication (lit_p2_lasthalfhour_summary.txt) finds outright last-half-hour momentum losing in the lowest volatility tercile from Jun 2020 to Nov 2026: NQ 136 trades at gross -3.55 bp per trade, ES 400 trades at gross -2.04 bp per trade. Over 2010-2026 it was close to zero. Hasbrouck's 15-minute convergence applies only to arbitrage-linked instruments on the same index and is deliberately not used as support.

### primary_rule
CONVENTIONS. Times are ET. 1-min bars are stamped at their open. P(hh:mm) is the close of the bar stamped one minute earlier, so P(14:30) is the close of the 14:29 bar. O(9:30) is the open of the 9:30 bar. 'Prior close' is the close of the previous full cash session's 15:59 bar (its 12:59 bar if that session was an early close), in the same contract. Instruments are MNQ (NQ price, $2/pt) and MES (ES price, $5/pt), using the front contract by volume as in data/{NQ,ES}_fut_1m.csv. Before May 2019, simulate micros on E-mini prices (same 0.25-pt tick). Compute every return from raw within-contract prices, never from the additively back-adjusted columns in daily_sessions_*.pkl; those distort early-sample returns (they give a 2010 daily NQ-on-ES beta of 0.54, against about 1.2 from raw intraday data).
ELIGIBLE DAY (all known by 14:30). (1) A full cash session: not an early close and not a Globex-only holiday. (2) Neither leg has changed contract since the prior cash session; this skips the first session after either leg rolls, which is 79 days in 2010-2026 because ES and NQ rolled on different days 12 times. (3) Both legs have the 9:30, 14:29 and 15:59 bars and no intraday gap over 10 minutes; this excludes 2020-03-09, 2020-03-12 and 2020-03-16. (4) Not a scheduled FOMC statement day (data/fomc_dates.csv) and not a CPI release day (data/cpi_dates.csv). (5) At least 60 prior full cash sessions are available.
HEDGE BETA b. OLS slope, with intercept, of 5-minute log returns of NQ on ES over the prior 60 full cash sessions (today excluded), on the grid 9:30-16:00: 78 returns per day, the first being P(9:35)/O(9:30).
SIGNAL at 14:30. e = ln(NQ P(14:30)/NQ O(9:30)) - b*ln(ES P(14:30)/ES O(9:30)). s = standard deviation of the same e over the prior 60 full cash sessions, each day's e computed with that day's own b. z = e/s.
FILTERS (all required). (F1) |z| >= 1.0. (F2) Calm tape: |ln(ES P(14:30)/ES prior close)| < 0.75%. (F3) Low-volatility regime: the VIX close (Yahoo) on the last trading day before today is below the median of the 252 VIX closes ending on that day. (F4) No overnight NQ-specific news: |zo| < 2, where zo = [ln(NQ O(9:30)/NQ prior close) - b*ln(ES O(9:30)/ES prior close)] divided by its standard deviation over the prior 60 eligible sessions. This stands in for mega-cap earnings after the prior close, because we have no earnings calendar.
POSITION (fade the residual). If z > 0, sell n_NQ MNQ and buy n_ES MES. If z < 0, buy n_NQ MNQ and sell n_ES MES. Sizing: k = b*(2*NQ P(14:30))/(5*ES P(14:30)). n_NQ is the smallest integer from 1 to 10 with |round(n_NQ*k) - n_NQ*k| <= 0.05*n_NQ*k; if none qualifies, take the one with the smallest relative error. n_ES = max(1, round(n_NQ*k)).
ENTRY: market orders on both legs at the open of the 14:30 bar, each filled 1 tick (0.25 pt) worse than that open.
EXIT: the 16:00 price (close of the 15:59 bar), each leg filled 1 tick worse. The only exit before that is a disaster stop. On each 1-min close from 14:30 to 15:58, recompute z_t with that close in place of P(14:30), using the same O(9:30), b and s. If z_t has the entry sign and |z_t| >= |z_entry| + 2.0, exit both legs at the next bar's open, 1 tick worse each. No profit target. At most one trade per day.
COSTS: n_NQ*$2.00 + n_ES*$3.50 per trade (1 tick each way plus $1.00 commission per micro round trip, on both legs). Our arithmetic gives about 11 bps of NQ-leg notional at 2010 prices, about 5 bps in 2018, about 3 bps in 2022 and under 2 bps in 2025-26.
PRIMARY STATISTIC: per-trade net return in bps of NQ-leg entry notional, = 1e4 * net $ / (n_NQ*2*NQ entry price).
MEASURED FREQUENCY (signal counts only, approximate beta, no P&L viewed): about 24 trades a year, at least 18 per 2-year block.

### variants
- V1, last half hour. Same filters, beta and sizing, but measured and entered at 15:30: z comes from the 9:30-to-15:30 residual (s from the same window), F2 uses ES P(15:30), entry is at the open of the 15:30 bar and exit at the 15:59 bar close, with the same disaster stop. This mirrors the window where our outright calm-day fade appeared. Measured: about 24 a year.
- V2, no VIX regime filter. The primary rule without F3 (calm tape only). It tests whether the low-volatility (long-gamma) regime matters. Measured: about 41 a year.

### trades_per_year
24

### evaluation
SAMPLE: about Sep 2010 to Nov 2026, after the 60-session burn-in. PERIODS: the full sample; Jun 2010 - May 2020 versus Jun 2020 - Nov 2026, the latter being after the May 2020 end of Baltussen et al., the latest academic sample on close-window index-futures behaviour cited here (Adams et al.'s sample end is unverified). Because our own calm-day fade was found in 2020-2026 data, that period is only partly out of sample. The true out-of-sample test is paper trading from 2026-11-09, at least 40 trades. 2-YEAR BLOCKS: 2010-11, 2012-13, ..., 2024-25 and 2026 (partial); a block counts only if it has at least 10 trades. CONTROLS: (C1, required) the same days, entry and exit bars and sizes with direction set by a fair coin, 1,000 draws with a fixed seed. (C2, diagnostic) eligible calm days with |z| < 1, faded the same way, to see whether the z gate adds anything. (C3, diagnostic) a regression of trade P&L on the ES return from 14:30 to 16:00, to confirm the residual market beta is under 0.1 in absolute value. PASS (all required): (a) event-level t (mean over standard error of per-trade net bps; also report Newey-West with 5 lags) >= 2.0 over the full sample; (b) mean net > 0 in Jun 2020 - Nov 2026; (c) mean net > 0 in at least 2/3 of counted 2-year blocks; (d) mean net above the 95th percentile of C1; (e) mean gross >= 1.5 x mean cost (house gate). Variants are extra trials and pass only with t >= 3 or a Holm-adjusted p < 0.05 across the 9 tests in this family. REPORT: results by z bucket (1-1.5, 1.5-2, >=2); by year; the number of stop exits; monthly-expiry Fridays separately; net results with costs held at 2026 bps (to show how much the high early-sample tick cost drives the outcome); daily P&L correlation with each existing model (chosen_models_trades.csv) and this spec's P&L on the days each existing model lost.

### evidence_after_review
weak

### overlap_with_existing
Low by design. It trades only on calm, low-VIX days with a small ES move by 14:30. Those are the chop days on which both MNQ breakouts lose or never trigger. It is beta-hedged, so it carries almost no market-direction risk. Between 14:30 and 16:00 its MNQ leg can sit against an open overnight-breakout or ORB position, because it fades NQ's move relative to ES while the breakout rides NQ's absolute move; on reversal days that partly hedges the breakout. It exits at 16:00, before the breakouts' 17:00 exit. It can trade on monthly-expiry Fridays next to the expiry fade (short MNQ from 9:30 to the close), so report those days separately. It needs margin for both micros at the same time as the existing positions.

### risks
There is no primary test. The support is S&P-only gamma evidence read through a secondary summary, plus our own outright result since 2020 from the same data, which is partly in sample. A residual gap may be information (intraday tech or mega-cap news) and keep trending; industry momentum (Hameed & Mian, to 2010) points that way. Two-leg costs at 2010-2016 index levels (about 5-11 bps per round trip) are large against a plausible reversion of a few bps, so the full-sample net test partly measures the early cost regime. With about 390 trades in total, splitting the sample by period halves the power. Integer micro rounding leaves up to 5% hedge error. The thresholds (|z| >= 1, 0.75%, VIX median) are fixed before any P&L run; the reviewed |z| >= 2 version gave only about 4.4 trades a year.

## S2 Monthly NQ-ES beta-neutral spread momentum (MNQ vs MES)

### mechanism
Factor and industry returns are positively autocorrelated over 1 to 12 months, and NQ minus beta*ES behaves like a growth/tech-tilt spread, so the spread's own trailing return may predict the sign of its next month. Ehsani & Linnainmaa (opened; Jul 1963 - Dec 2015) report a pooled slope on a prior-year-positive dummy of 0.52 (t = 4.67). For US size the slope is 0.70 (t = 2.76): SMB earns 55 bps a month after a positive year and -15 bps after a negative one. US value is 0.28 (t = 1.16) and global size 0.23 (t = 0.95), neither significant. Their Table A2 shows time-series factor momentum earning 0.36% a month (t = 6.77) with a 12-month formation period and 0.35% a month (t = 6.61) with a 1-month formation period, both held one month. Hameed & Mian (to Dec 2010; reviewer-confirmed) find the inter-industry component averages -0.30% a month, which means industry momentum. Falck, Rej & Thesmar (arXiv 2009.04824, abstract opened) find factor momentum adds to stock momentum only in implementations that include the most recent month. Carrying factor results over to an index-futures spread is an extrapolation.

### primary_rule
SIGNAL (known at the previous month's close). On the last trading day of month m, use Yahoo daily closes of the Nasdaq-100 (^NDX) and S&P 500 (^GSPC) price indexes. b_m = OLS slope of daily log returns of NDX on SPX over the 250 trading days ending that day. The monthly spread return is R_j = ln(NDX_j/NDX_j-1) - b_j-1*ln(SPX_j/SPX_j-1), using month-end closes. S12_m = R_m-11 + ... + R_m. Direction d = sign(S12_m): +1 means long NQ and short ES; if S12_m is exactly 0, stay flat. Cash indexes are used so the signal needs no futures burn-in and avoids back-adjustment errors.
POSITION for month m+1. At the open of the 10:00 1-min bar of the first cash session of month m+1, open d x (+n_NQ MNQ, -n_ES MES), each leg filled 1 tick worse. Sizing is as in S1, with k = b_m*2*NQ/(5*ES) at that bar's prices and n_NQ the smallest integer from 1 to 10 whose MES rounding error is at most 5%. Close both legs at the open of the 10:00 bar of the first cash session of month m+2, 1 tick worse each, and immediately open month m+2's position. Re-establishing the whole position every month, and charging both legs a round trip even when the direction is unchanged, is deliberately conservative.
ROLLS (never span two contracts). If either leg's front contract in the 1-min files changes during the holding month, close both legs at the 15:59 bar close (12:59 on an early close) of the last cash session on which both legs are still in the held contracts. Stay flat, then reopen both legs with the same direction and the sizing rule at new prices at the open of the 10:00 bar of the first cash session on which both legs are in the new contracts. Charge both round trips.
HOLIDAYS AND EARLY CLOSES: entry and exit dates are cash sessions only. If the 10:00 bar is missing, use the open of the next available 1-min bar.
COSTS: n_NQ*$2.00 + n_ES*$3.50 per round trip, for every monthly re-establishment and every roll. That is about 11 bps a month at 2010 prices and under 2 bps now.
STATISTIC: monthly net return in bps of NQ-leg notional for a unit position with no volatility targeting. For live use only (reported, not tested): scale to 8% annualized spread volatility using the trailing 60-session standard deviation of daily beta-hedged spread returns, capped at 2x the unit size.
MEASURED SIGNAL PROPERTIES (no P&L viewed): over Jun 2010 - Oct 2026 the 12-month sign was positive in 73% of months and changed 23 times.

### variants
- V1, 1-month lookback: d = sign(R_m), last month's spread return only, with the same execution. Supported by Ehsani & Linnainmaa Table A2 (1-month formation, t = 6.61, pooled factors to 2015) and by Falck, Rej & Thesmar (only lags that include the latest month survive spanning). The 1-month sign changed 90 times in 2010-2026.
- V2, RTY-ES: the same rule with the Russell 2000 (^RUT) in place of NDX in the signal and M2K ($5/pt, tick 0.1 pt) in place of MNQ. P&L comes from CME RTY hourly bars (data/RTY_fut_1h.csv): entry and exit at the open of the 10:00 hourly bar, roll exits at the close of the 15:00 hourly bar, with ES 1-min bars at the same timestamps. Only Jul 2017 - Nov 2026 counts (about 112 months); an index-only proxy for 2010-2017 is reported but not counted. The M2K cost of $2.00 per round trip ($0.50 tick each way plus $1.00) is an assumption, not a given. The closest factor is US size (slope 0.70, t = 2.76), but global size is not significant (t = 0.95). The 12-month RUT-SPX sign changed 24 times in 2010-2026.

### trades_per_year
12

### evaluation
SAMPLE: Jul 2010 - Nov 2026, about 196 holding months; the cash-index signal loses nothing to burn-in. PERIOD AFTER THE SOURCES: Jan 2016 - Nov 2026, after Ehsani & Linnainmaa's Dec 2015 end; Hameed & Mian end in 2010, so the whole sample is after them. 2-YEAR BLOCKS: 2010-11, ..., 2024-25 and 2026 (partial); a block counts only if it has at least 10 months. CONTROLS: (C1, required) circular-shift placebo: shift the monthly direction series by every offset from 12 to N-12 months (a full deterministic enumeration) and recompute the mean net; the real mean must exceed the 95th percentile. This keeps the share of long months and the signal's persistence but breaks its timing. (C2, required) static benchmark: always long NQ and short b*ES, with identical execution and costs; the strategy's mean net must exceed it, because NQ beat ES for most of 2010-2026 and a mostly-long signal can look good from drift alone. PASS: (a) month-level t on net returns (plain and Newey-West with 3 lags) >= 2.0 over the full sample; (b) mean net > 0 in Jan 2016 - Nov 2026; (c) mean net > 0 in at least 2/3 of counted 2-year blocks; (d) C1 and C2 as above; (e) mean net still > 0 after dropping the single best sign regime, because about 23 sign flips mean a few regimes can carry the whole result. Variants pass only with t >= 3 or Holm across the family. DIAGNOSTIC (not a gate): the same signal on cash-index spreads over 1990-2009, gross, as an earlier-period check; it lies inside the factor papers' samples. In that period NDX-SPX had 37 sign changes and RUT-SPX 26.

### evidence_after_review
weak

### overlap_with_existing
It does not depend on intraday trend or chop. It holds a beta-neutral spread for a month, so its daily P&L is the NQ-minus-b*ES residual, not market direction. The three existing models are outright MNQ positions, and that residual is part of their P&L, so expect a small positive daily correlation when S2 is long the spread and a small negative one when it is short. Report daily P&L correlation with each model and the extra margin. It shares no entry times with them (10:00 at month start, versus intraday breakout triggers and the third-Friday expiry fade).

### risks
The factor evidence ends in 2015 and concerns long-short stock factors, not index futures. The closest factor to NQ-ES (value/growth) is not significant (t = 1.16). 12-month factor momentum is spanned by stock momentum (Falck, Rej & Thesmar). About 23 sign changes in 16 years amount to roughly a dozen independent bets, and reaching t = 2 over 16 years needs a Sharpe ratio of about 0.5 (t = SR x sqrt(years)). Strong one-way NQ outperformance can pass for skill; C1, C2 and the drop-best-regime rule address that. Holding for a month carries tail risk from single mega-cap events (earnings, antitrust) in NQ. Index-based signals differ slightly from futures (carry and dividends); the error is small at monthly horizons.

## S3 Big-day close-flow spread continuation, 15:30 to 16:00 (MNQ vs MES)

### mechanism
Leveraged-ETF swap counterparties and short-gamma hedgers must trade in the direction of the day's move near the close. Baltussen et al. (opened; Dec 1974 - May 2020) find pooled equity-index futures (17 markets worldwide, including ES, NQ, YM and Russell) have a last-half-hour slope on the rest-of-day return of 4.18 (t = 7.29, R2 2.45%, out-of-sample R2 2.88%). Slopes in that table are multiplied by 100, so a 1% move predicts about 4 bps, gross; the authors 'do not consider transaction costs'. The LETF-share x rest-of-day interaction is 1.79 (t = 2.20) to 2.94 (t = 3.10) over Jun 2006 - May 2020. Barbon et al. (single stocks, 2012-2019; reviewer-confirmed) find LETF rebalancing happens only at the end of the day and about a third of its impact reverses at the next open. The spread version also needs Nasdaq-100 LETF demand to be larger than the S&P 500's relative to the index. That premise is unverified (a search snippet only), and no source estimates an NQ-minus-ES differential.

### primary_rule
Instruments, timestamp conventions, hedge beta b (5-minute, prior 60 sessions), integer sizing, fills and costs are exactly as in S1.
ELIGIBLE DAY: a full cash session (no early close or Globex-only holiday); no roll in either leg since the prior cash session; the 15:29 and 15:59 bars present; at least 60 prior sessions. FOMC and CPI days are not excluded but are reported separately. PRECEDENCE: skip any day on which S1's primary entry conditions (F1-F4 on an eligible S1 day) were met at 14:30, whether or not S1 traded.
SIGNAL at 15:30. R_NQ = ln(NQ P(15:30)/NQ prior close) and R_ES = ln(ES P(15:30)/ES prior close), with P(15:30) the close of the 15:29 bar and the prior close as defined in S1. Gate: |R_NQ| >= 1.0% AND |R_ES| >= 1.0% AND same sign.
POSITION (continuation). On an up day buy n_NQ MNQ and sell n_ES MES; on a down day sell n_NQ MNQ and buy n_ES MES. k uses b and the 15:30 prices.
ENTRY: open of the 15:30 bar, each leg 1 tick worse. EXIT: close of the 15:59 bar, each leg 1 tick worse. No stop and no target.
COSTS: n_NQ*$2.00 + n_ES*$3.50 per trade.
STATISTIC: per-trade net bps of NQ-leg notional, tested two-sided. A significantly negative mean is reported as evidence for a fade but does not count as a pass; a fade would need its own pre-registration and forward test.
MEASURED FREQUENCY (no P&L viewed): about 49 a year, at least 40 per 2-year block, with 47% of signal days after Jun 2020. Before the precedence rule, 2 days in 2010-2026 overlapped S1.

### variants
- V1, larger-move gate: |R_NQ| >= 1.5% and |R_ES| >= 1.5%, same sign (about 24 a year measured). Rebalancing demand grows with the size of the day's move: Cheng & Madhavan, cited by Baltussen et al., put it at 16.8% of market-on-close volume on a 1% move day and 50.2% on a 5% move day (Feb 2009). The effect should therefore rise with |R|.
- V2, the research's original gate: |R_NQ| >= 1.0% with R_ES of the same sign and any size, under the S1 precedence rule (about 78 a year measured).

### trades_per_year
49

### evaluation
SAMPLE: about Sep 2010 - Nov 2026. PERIOD AFTER THE SOURCES: Jun 2020 - Nov 2026, after Baltussen et al.'s May 2020 end; Barbon et al. end in Dec 2019. 2-YEAR BLOCKS as in S1; a block counts only with at least 10 trades. CONTROLS: (C1, required) the same days and bars with random direction, 1,000 draws with a fixed seed. (C2, required placebo) the same days and direction rule but held from 14:30 to 15:00, before the roughly 30-minute pre-close hedging window that Baltussen et al. cite from Shum et al. The 15:30-16:00 mean must exceed the placebo mean; otherwise the result is not a close-flow effect. PASS: (a) event-level t >= 2 on net returns over the full sample; (b) mean net > 0 in Jun 2020 - Nov 2026; (c) mean net > 0 in at least 2/3 of counted blocks; (d) mean above the 95th percentile of C1; (e) the C2 condition; (f) mean gross >= 1.5 x cost. Variants pass only with t >= 3 or Holm across the family. REPORT: results by |R| bucket and by VIX tercile; FOMC and CPI days separately; the outright MNQ and MES last-half-hour moves on the same days, to show whether any spread return just reflects NQ's higher beta; and net results with costs held at 2026 bps.

### evidence_after_review
weak

### overlap_with_existing
High with the two breakouts. It trades only on big same-direction days, which are their trend days, and it adds a long-NQ/short-ES tilt in the day's direction while they already hold MNQ in that direction until 17:00. That concentrates risk rather than diversifying it, and it does nothing for chop days. On big down monthly-expiry Fridays it adds to the expiry fade's short MNQ. The precedence rule makes it mutually exclusive with S1.

### risks
No source estimates the NQ-minus-ES differential. The pooled outright effect is only about 4 bps per 1% move, gross and to 2020, so the spread differential is a fraction of that, while a two-leg round trip costs about 1.6-2 bps now and about 5-11 bps before 2018. It will probably fail net of costs. Ivanov & Lenkey (2006-2014) find the late-day LETF effect 'economically insignificant' once capital flows are counted, and Lenkey's 2024 survey agrees (not independent). Nasdaq-100 mega-caps are large S&P 500 weights, so LETF flows lift both legs. Our own outright replication found no gross last-half-hour momentum from Jun 2020 to Nov 2026 (variant a: ES -0.08 bp per trade, NQ -0.41 bp per trade).
