## Summary from the research stage
Nothing in this family has direct, cost-adjusted evidence in index futures, so all three specs are rated weak and are built to fail cleanly. The best candidate is Spec A, a calm-day 60-minute fade on MNQ. It rests on Adams et al.: in ES from 2018 to 2024, hour-ahead 'momentum returns' turned negative when dealer gamma was high (-1.144 bps at 60 min, t=-1.97; Q4-Q1 -1.777, t=-3.93; re-read in the PDF). That result is gross, measured within each day and unsigned, and a prior-close VIX filter can only roughly stand in for it; unconditionally, hour-scale futures returns trend weakly. Spec B fades real-time drift bursts on MES. Its basis is ES 2010-2017 evidence that 67-71% of bursts reverse, but that rate is measured from a peak only known in hindsight, and pooled evidence shows strong 2-16-minute moves tend to continue. Spec C turns the failed-ORB idea into a stop-and-reverse exit rule on the existing ORB, as the reviewer advised. The VWAP-band fade survives only as a variant of A. The RSI filter, the 120-minute hold and the market-neutral pair idea were dropped.

## A. Calm-day 60-minute fade (MNQ)

### mechanism
Hypothesis: when options dealers are net long gamma, their delta hedging sells rallies and buys dips, so on calm days large hour-scale index moves partly revert. Support I verified in the PDF is Adams et al. (ES, RTH 9:30-16:00, Jan 2018 - Dec 2024). Dealer net gamma was positive in 88% of 10-min intervals and averaged 0.88% of the S&P before 19 May 2022 vs 1.42% after. In the top quartile of a 180-day rolling gamma ranking, the mean of R(t,t+n) - R(t-n-1,t-1) is -0.606 bps at 30m (t=-1.34), -1.144 at 60m (t=-1.97) and -2.158 at 120m (t=-2.46). Q4-Q1 is -1.055/-1.777/-2.888 (t=-2.99/-3.93/-4.18). These are gross, unsigned future-minus-past differences estimated with Date and Year x Week x Time fixed effects, not trade P&L. The authors say gamma is unobservable, so this is not a feasible strategy as published. From Safari-Schmidhuber (pooled futures 2010-2023, verified): at 1 hour to 1 day, weak moves trend (b=+0.132%, t=2.9) and strong moves revert (c=-0.039%, t=-2.8), so only moves of 1 sigma or more are faded. From Baltussen et al. (Jan 1996 - May 2020, verified): with S&P net gamma >= 0 the last-half-hour momentum beta is 0.82 (t=1.03), vs 6.63 (t=4.78) when negative. Prior-close VIX and a small first hour are only public stand-ins for 'calm'; the vendor GEX study does not show that low VIX identifies long gamma (reviewer: overstated). Also consistent with the trader's own finding that the last-half-hour fade worked on calm days since 2020.

### primary_rule
Instrument: 1 MNQ. Signals and simulated fills use NQ 1-min bars stamped at their open, with all times America/New_York. 'Price at HH:MM' = close of the bar stamped one minute earlier (the 10:30 price is the close of the 10:29 bar). CONTRACT: on day D, use only the NQ contract id with the largest 9:30-15:59 volume on the prior NYSE trading day. Positions are flat by 15:00, so no trade spans two contracts. Trailing statistics use each past day's own contract and within-day returns only. ELIGIBLE DAY (everything known by 10:30): an NYSE full trading day that is not an NYSE early-close day (CME equity close 13:15 ET), not a scheduled FOMC statement day and not a monthly options-expiry Friday (third Friday, or Thursday if Friday is a holiday). It must also pass both filters: (1) VIX close on D-1 <= the median of the 252 VIX closes ending D-1; (2) first-hour range H1 = (max high - min low of bars 9:30-10:29) / open of the 9:30 bar <= the median H1 of the prior 20 NYSE full trading days. DECISION TIMES: T = 11:00, 11:30, 12:00, 12:30, 13:00, 13:30, 14:00, checked only when flat. P(T) = close of bar T-1. dP = P(T) - P(T-60). r60 = ln(P(T)/P(T-60)). s60(T) = root-mean-square of r60 at the same clock time over the prior 20 NYSE full trading days. SIGNAL: |r60| >= 1.0 x s60(T). ENTRY: market at the open of bar T, direction = -sign(dP). EXITS, whichever comes first: (a) target limit at P(T) - 0.5 x dP, filled only if a bar from the entry bar onward trades through it by >= 1 tick; (b) stop-market at entry - |dP| for longs or entry + |dP| for shorts, filled at the stop or at the bar open if the bar opens beyond it; (c) market exit at the open of bar T+60; (d) hard flat at the open of the 15:00 bar. If one bar touches both target and stop, assume the stop. Exits on a bar are processed before any new entry on that bar. COSTS: 1.0 NQ point ($2.00) per MNQ round trip (1 tick slippage each way + $1 commission). Stress test (report only): +2 ticks on stop fills. Record P&L in NQ points, $ per MNQ and net bps of entry price.

### variants
- A1, the instrument the evidence covers: identical rule computed on ES 1-min bars and traded as 1 MES. Cost 0.70 ES point ($3.50) per round trip.
- A2, VWAP trigger (the only surviving form of the dropped VWAP-band fade): same days, decision times, one-position rule and exits (c)/(d). Signal when |P(T) - VWAP(T)| >= 2.0 x S(T). VWAP(T) = volume-weighted average of the typical price (H+L+C)/3 over bars 9:30 to T-1. S(T) = mean |P - VWAP| at clock time T over the prior 14 NYSE full trading days. Enter at market at the open of bar T, toward VWAP. Target: limit at VWAP(T), frozen at T, same trade-through fill rule. Stop: entry -/+ |P(T) - VWAP(T)|. No limit-at-band entries.

### trades_per_year
70

### evaluation
Sample: first eligible day after the 20-day warm-up (Jul 2010) through the latest data (Nov 2026). Micros listed only in 2019, so pre-2019 results are hypothetical micro P&L. REPORT-ONLY SPLITS: Jun 2010 - Dec 2017; 1 Jan 2018 - 18 May 2022; 19 May 2022 (daily SPXW expiries, per Adams) - Dec 2024; Jan 2025 - latest (after the Adams sample, which ends Dec 2024). TWO-YEAR BLOCKS (8): Jun 2010-2011, 2012-13, 2014-15, 2016-17, 2018-19, 2020-21, 2022-23, Jan 2024 - Nov 2026. CONTROLS: C1, random direction: same entry bars, same target/stop distances and time exits, direction set by a fair coin, 1,000 replications. C2 (report only): the same fade rule on full days that fail the VIX or H1 filter, to show whether the calm filter matters. PASS (all required, net of costs): (i) day-level t >= 2.0 on daily net bps (days with at least one trade; Newey-West, 5 lags) over 2010-2026; (ii) total net P&L > 0 from Jan 2025 to latest; (iii) net P&L > 0 in at least 6 of the 8 blocks; (iv) mean net bps per trade above the 95th percentile of C1. Gross P&L by block is also reported, so a failure caused only by early-year costs is visible; the criterion is not relaxed. A variant replaces the primary only if it passes (i)-(iv) with t >= 2.4 (3 tests). DIAGNOSTICS: hit rate, exit-type mix, points per trade by era, daily P&L correlation with the three existing strategies.

### evidence_after_review
weak

### overlap_with_existing
Strategy 1 (overnight-range breakout, held to 17:00) and strategy 2 (5-min ORB, held to the close) trade the same hours on many of the same days. When A fades a move in a breakout's direction, the combined MNQ book is partly flat for up to 60 minutes. A trades only calm days, where the breakouts tend to lose, so its daily P&L correlation with both should be negative. Report the correlation, the share of A trades taken while each breakout is open (same vs opposite direction), and combined drawdown. Strategy 3 (monthly opex Friday fade): no overlap, because A skips those days. The trader's calm-day last-half-hour fade (a research finding, not one of the three strategies) does not overlap in time because A is flat by 15:00, but both depend on the same calm regime and would fail together if it breaks.

### risks
Proxy dilution: Adams' effect comes from variation within each day (date fixed effects absorb differences between days), and a prior-close VIX cannot track intraday gamma. Counter-evidence: Safari-Schmidhuber's hour-scale trend coefficients roughly agree across asset classes, including equities (Fig. 6, shown graphically only). NQ had among the strongest last-half-hour momentum through May 2020 (beta_ROD 6.36, t=7.97, vs ES 6.18, t=4.97; Baltussen Table B1), and no source measures NDX dealer gamma. Costs in bps were much higher earlier: 1 NQ point is about 1.4 bps at 2018 prices and roughly 4-6 bps at 2010-11 prices (approximate, from price levels), so the early blocks may fail on costs alone. Gamma flips (Feb 2018, Mar 2020, Apr 2025) hurt fades most, and a VIX filter lagged one day reacts late. Stop fills in fast markets slip more than 1 tick. The 0.5x target and 1.0x stop are untuned and must not be tuned after seeing results.

## B. Midday drift-burst fade (MES)

### mechanism
Hypothesis: sudden explosive moves driven by forced or one-sided flow (stop cascades, hedging, liquidity withdrawal) overshoot and partly revert once liquidity returns. Verified in Christensen-Oomen-Renò (ES Jan 2010 - Dec 2017; 5-second mid-quotes; backward-looking exponential kernel; drift bandwidth 5 min, volatility bandwidth about 25 min). At critical value 4.0: 549 ES bursts; regressing post-burst on pre-burst 5-min return gives b=-0.26 (t=-12.05, R2 21.0%), and 67.2% reverse. At 4.5: 202 bursts (about one every two weeks), b=-0.36 (t=-14.45, R2 51.0%), 70.8% reverse. Excluding the top decile, b falls to -0.14 (4.0) and -0.20 (4.5). With at most one event per day at cv 4.0: 253 negative bursts averaged -37.39 bps before and +8.01 bps after; 208 positive bursts averaged +36.41 before and -5.44 after. Serial correlation is -79.58% (negative bursts) vs -25.73% (positive). High-volume negative bursts reverse more (+13.29 vs +4.89 bps). Caveats: the event time is the local extremum of the statistic, which needs hindsight. The post window is 5 min in Eq. 35, although the text once says 55 minutes. The paper has no trading or cost results. This spec tests whether a real-time version, entering 1 minute after the peak, survives.

### primary_rule
Instrument: 1 MES. Signals and fills use ES 1-min bars of day D's contract (largest prior-day 9:30-15:59 volume). Positions are flat by 14:45, so no trade spans two contracts. ELIGIBLE DAYS: NYSE full trading days, excluding early-close days, scheduled FOMC statement days and monthly opex Fridays. No VIX filter. STATISTIC (my 1-minute proxy for the source's test): r_i = ln(C_i / C_(i-1)) from the day contract's 1-min closes starting 08:00 ET (a missing bar gives r=0). At each bar close t: D_t = sum over j=0..29 of exp(-j/5) x r_(t-j); V_t = [sum over j=0..119 of exp(-j/25) x r_(t-j)^2] / [sum over j=0..119 of exp(-j/25)]; T_t = D_t / sqrt(V_t x sum over j=0..29 of exp(-2j/5)). ARM: the first bar t stamped 10:29-14:29 with |T_t| >= 4.0; set s = sign(T_t). PEAK: the first bar t' > t with s x T_t' < s x T_(t'-1); the peak bar is p = t'-1. ENTRY: market at the open of bar t'+1, direction -s, only if that bar is stamped 14:30 or earlier; otherwise no trade. M = |C_p - C_(p-5)| in points (the pre-burst 5-min move). STOP: stop-market at entry - M for longs or entry + M for shorts, filled at the stop or at a worse bar open. TIME EXIT: market at the open of the bar 15 minutes after the entry bar. No target. At most one event per day (first arming only, no re-arming). If one bar touches the stop and the time exit is due, assume the stop. COSTS: 0.70 ES point ($3.50) per MES round trip. Stress (report only): +2 ticks on stop fills.

### variants
- B1, long-only: trade only negative bursts (s = -1), the side with the strongest reversal in the source. Its control adds a drift check: long entries at random bars in the same window on eligible days, with the same 15-min hold and stop distances drawn from the events' M distribution.
- B2, instrument: identical rule on NQ 1-min bars traded as 1 MNQ. Cost 1.0 NQ point ($2.00) per round trip.

### trades_per_year
20

### evaluation
Sample: Jun 2010 to latest. 2010-2017 overlaps the source's ES sample, and Jan 2018 onward is after it. CALIBRATION, done before looking at P&L: report the event count and the mean pre-burst move in bps, and compare with the source's ES means of about -37/+36 bps. The 4.0 threshold is fixed and never re-tuned. Fewer than 200 events in total means the test is underpowered and counts as a fail. CONTROLS: C1, random direction: same entry bars, same stop distance and 15-min exit, 1,000 replications. C2 (diagnostic): enter at the arming bar instead of after the peak, to show how much the result depends on peak timing. PASS (net of costs): (i) event-level t >= 2.0 (one event per day) over 2010-2026; (ii) net P&L > 0 from Jan 2018 to latest (the source's ES sample ends Dec 2017); (iii) net > 0 in at least 6 of the 8 two-year blocks (same blocks as Spec A); (iv) mean net bps above C1's 95th percentile. A variant is adopted only with t >= 2.4. REPORT-ONLY SPLITS: calm (VIX <= 252-day median) vs not; negative vs positive bursts; with vs without the largest 10% of events by M.

### evidence_after_review
weak

### overlap_with_existing
It trades a different instrument (MES) from all three existing MNQ strategies. A burst in the direction of an open overnight-range or ORB position is faded for 15 minutes, a small, brief offset to those positions on trend days; report daily P&L correlation and simultaneous opposite exposure. No overlap with the opex Friday fade, because those days are skipped. It can fire on the same days as Spec A; report the overlap if both run.

### risks
Counter-evidence: Safari-Schmidhuber (pooled futures 2010-2023) find that at 2-16 minutes strong moves continue (c=+0.259%, t=+4.6), even though weak moves revert (b=-0.912%, t=-13.6). The source's reversal is concentrated in the largest events (the slope roughly halves without the top decile, and the ES sample was started in 2010 to include the May 2010 flash crash), so a handful of events can dominate. My 1-min proxy statistic differs from the source's pre-averaged statistic on 5-second mid-quotes, so events will not match one-for-one. Fading crashes has a fat left tail: stops slip, and trading halts occurred in Mar 2020. There are few events (about 15-30 a year, a rough guess), so statistical power is low. 2010-2017 lies inside the source's sample.

## C. ORB failed-breakout stop-and-reverse (exit rule on the existing MNQ ORB)

### mechanism
Hypothesis only: on choppy days the ORB breakout fails and price returns through the opening range as breakout traders exit. Under positive dealer gamma (positive in 88% of 10-min intervals in Adams' ES 2018-2024 sample, verified), moves away from the open meet hedging that pushes back. No primary source tests a failed 5-min ORB. The nearest evidence is indirect: reversals after large opening moves in S&P futures (Grant-Wolf-Yu, Nov 1987 - Sep 2002, abstract only, with significance dropping sharply after bid-ask costs; Fung-Mok-Lam, abstract only, profits after costs modest) and ES drift-burst reversals (2010-2017, measured from a peak only known in hindsight). Following the reviewer: a separate reverse model held against an ORB that runs to the close just flattens the book, so the idea is tested as a change to the ORB's exit.

### primary_rule
Base: the trader's existing MNQ 5-min ORB exactly as currently coded (entry, 1 MNQ, exit at the close). C changes only what happens after a failed breakout. OR = high/low of NQ 1-min bars 9:30-9:34 of day D's contract (the ORB's own range); mid = (ORH+ORL)/2; W = ORH - ORL. APPLIES only when the ORB holds a position in direction d (+1 long, -1 short) entered at or before 10:55, on days that are not early-close days, scheduled FOMC statement days or monthly opex Fridays. FAILURE TEST: build 5-min bars from 1-min bars stamped 9:35, 9:40, ..., 10:55; each covers stamps t to t+4 and closes at the close of bar t+4. Starting with the 5-min bar that contains the ORB entry bar, failure is the first 5-min bar that closes on the far side of mid (d=+1: close < mid; d=-1: close > mid). ACTION at the open of the 1-min bar right after that 5-min bar closes (stamp f+5): exit the ORB position at market and enter 1 MNQ in direction -d at market. REVERSE-LEG STOP: for d=+1, the highest high of 1-min bars from the ORB entry bar through the failure bar, + 2 ticks (0.50 pt); for d=-1, the lowest low - 0.50 pt. Stop-market. REVERSE-LEG EXIT: market at the open of the 14:00 bar, or the stop. No target. After a failure there are no further ORB-family trades that day; suppress any later ORB re-entry in every compared book. If the ORB's own stop (if it has one) is hit first, there is no failure event. COSTS: the ORB exits once either way, so its cost is unchanged. The reverse leg pays 1.0 NQ point ($2.00) per round trip. Stress (report only): +2 ticks on stops.

### variants
- C1, calm-only reverse leg: open the reverse leg only when the VIX close on D-1 is at or below its 252-day median. On other days the ORB still exits at the failure signal (plain exit).
- C2, with target: add a reverse-leg limit target at ORL - 0.5 x W (failed long) or ORH + 0.5 x W (failed short), using the trade-through-by-1-tick fill rule.

### trades_per_year
60

### evaluation
Sample: Jun 2010 to latest, on the ORB's own backtest days. Three books on identical days: B0, the baseline ORB; B1 (control), the ORB with a plain exit at the failure signal and no reverse leg; B2, the ORB with stop-and-reverse. B2 - B1 is the reverse leg's P&L. CONTROLS: B1, plus a random-direction reverse leg at the same entry bars with the same stop distance and 14:00 exit (1,000 replications). PASS (net of costs): (i) reverse-leg event-level t >= 2.0 over 2010-2026 (one event per day); (ii) reverse-leg net > 0 from Jan 2018 to latest (no source covers failed ORBs, and the nearest, Christensen ES, ends Dec 2017); also report Jan 2025 to latest; (iii) reverse-leg net > 0 in at least 6 of the 8 two-year blocks; (iv) mean net above the random-direction 95th percentile; (v) book test: mean daily (B2 - B0) > 0, and B2's maximum drawdown <= B0's. A variant is adopted only with t >= 2.4. Report B1 - B0 separately, to show whether the early exit alone helps or hurts the ORB.

### evidence_after_review
weak

### overlap_with_existing
It is entirely a modification of existing strategy 2 (5-min ORB), and its P&L is mechanically tied to ORB failure days, so judge it only at the book level. With strategy 1 (overnight-range breakout): when strategy 1 holds the same direction as the failed ORB, the reverse leg offsets it until 14:00; report how often this happens. No overlap with strategy 3, because opex Fridays are skipped.

### risks
There is no direct evidence. The failure signal can fire on noise, and the tight stop near the post-breakout extreme can be hit repeatedly on whipsaw days. The early exit may cut ORB winners that break out again later (visible in B1 - B0). It interacts with details of the existing ORB code (its own stop and re-entry rules) that this spec cannot see. Data-snooping risk if the trader has already eyeballed failed breakouts. Each failure costs an extra round trip. The 14:00 exit and the 2-tick stop buffer are inherited from R3, not tuned, and must stay fixed.
