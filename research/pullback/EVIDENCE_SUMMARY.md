Sorry this took a while. The evidence was long and the spec has to be exact enough for two programmers to get identical trades. The evidence offers little support for the trader's specific process; the default expectation is no edge after costs. Simple moving-average rules looked strong in-sample (Brock-Lakonishok-LeBaron, DJIA 1897-1986) but failed the data-snooping test: the best of 7,846 rules had p = 0.341 for 1987-96 and p = 0.908 on S&P futures (Sullivan-Timmermann-White). Intraday 5-minute rules on SPY showed nothing after correction. Momentum(14) > 100 is the same thing as a rising 14-bar average, so it adds nothing to the EMA filter. Monthly momentum has an out-of-sample R2 of only about 0.1-0.15% (Neely et al.). What does hold up is late-day intraday momentum in RTH: on ES, the return up to 15:45 predicts the last half-hour with R2 1.48% (Baltussen); the 1/N equity-index futures portfolio earned Sharpe 1.73 gross, and S&P futures net Sharpe stays positive at a 1-tick cost but is not quantified. SPY timing earns 4.46%/yr net of spread (Gao). All these effects are 3-5x stronger on high-volatility days. No study tests intraday pullback entries in index futures. Dip-buying at the monthly horizon lowers returns (9.1% vs 11.0% a year after negative months, Estrada). Support/resistance levels beat random levels by only about 4-5 points (60.8% vs 56.2% bounces, Osler FX). 'Today repeats yesterday' is contradicted: yesterday's last half-hour predicts today's NEGATIVELY in equity futures (coefficient about -6.7, t about -2.7). Averaging down has no support. A breakeven stop after adding works only when adding to a winner, so variant Ac refuses adds where the breakeven price would sit at or above the market. A Monte Carlo of the eval rules suggests that, with a modest edge (daily Sharpe 0.2), the pass rate falls from about 72% at $150 daily sd to 38% at $600, so bigger size lowers pass odds; it gives about 20-28% with no edge, but that figure is understated by a 60-day cap. Two rules are still unconfirmed: whether Lucid's consistency rule is 40% or 50%, and whether the $600 daily limit exists. The spec runs both as sensitivities. The spec fixes 121 variants (101 strategy, 20 analog). The primary is S040, the trader's stated settings: 15-minute bars, EMA 8/20/50, Momentum 14, swing pivots, no add, stop below the swing low. Every variant is checked with a 3-year trailing walk-forward from 2013, neighbour stability, a 1,000-draw random-entry control (seed 12345), a family-wise best-of-N test and a cross-market check on ES/NQ/YM. Eval pass odds come from root prop_sim.py, set against the existing plans (80%/133 days and 58%/47 days).
